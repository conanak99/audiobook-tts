# Deciding who speaks a Vietnamese quote

These conventions cover the user's stories: a male first-person narrator and mostly one heroine, in xianxia or modern settings.

## Narrator and pronouns

| Setting | Narrator refers to himself as | Narration refers to her as | She calls him | He calls her |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Xianxia | `ta` | `nàng` (sometimes `muội ấy`, `tỷ`) | `sư huynh`, `huynh`, `chàng`, `phu quân`, `tướng công`; she calls herself `muội`, `thiếp`, `ta` | `muội`, `nàng`, `nương tử`, a pet name (`Tiểu Nguyệt`, `Nguyệt nhi`) |
| Modern | `tôi` | `cô`, `cô ấy`, `em`, `ẻm`, `chị`, `dì`, or by name | `anh`; she calls herself `em`. With an older woman: he is `em`, she is `chị` | `em`, by name, `cô` (formal) |

- `anh` and `em` are used by **both** speakers, as "I" and as "you". Use the meaning, not the word. For example, in `"Em đang ghen à?"` the speaker is addressing her, so "em" means "you".
- `muội` is also both: "I" (she speaks) or "you" (he speaks).
- `ta` in dialogue is a self-reference that both men and women use in xianxia; it does not prove the narrator is speaking.
- `nàng ta`, `cô ta`, `hắn` and `anh ta` in a quote refer to a third person.

## Strong cues

1. A speech tag in the same paragraph, before or after the quote: `"…," nàng đáp`, `Ta hé mắt. "…"`, `"…" Em liếc tôi.` One paragraph is one speaker, except for an explicit second tag.
2. A vocative names the listener, not the speaker: `"Ngủ ngon, Zinie."` is not Zinie speaking.
3. A reply answers the previous line. Questions get answers, and commands get reactions.
4. In a two-person exchange without tags, speakers alternate. Recount the alternation whenever an action beat in between names who acted.

## Not dialogue → `narrator`

These are not dialogue: quoted terms or titles inside narration, written signs or messages that the narrator reads aloud, onomatopoeia (`"bụp"`), and the narrator's remembered words when he is retelling them rather than speaking.

## New people

Minor characters (elders, maids, a mother, a rival) get `new: <Name or role> | male|female`. Use the name the text uses (`Triệu trưởng lão`, `mẹ tôi`, `bé An`). If two names refer to the same person (`Sương Nguyệt` and `Nguyệt nhi`), use the one already in the speaker list.

## Gender questions (`SPEAKER X`)

Decide from titles (`sư tỷ`/`phu nhân` = female; `sư huynh`/`công tử`/`lão` = male), from the pronouns that refer back to the name (`nàng`, `cô ấy` = female; `hắn`, `anh ấy`, `gã` = male), from `-chan`/`-kun`, and from how the person speaks. `trưởng lão` and `môn chủ` are not gendered.
